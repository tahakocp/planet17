# Assembly

Assumes r4. For r5 the only difference is four planets. Untested on a real print, so read it as the intended order, not a field-proven one.

Before you start, clean elephant foot off the plates and the gear bores, and test-fit one planet on an M3 screw. It should spin freely with no wobble. If it binds, raise `fit` and reprint the planets.

1. Fix the housing to the motor with four M3 x 6 screws through the counterbores in the base. The motor pilot goes into the large hole.
2. Slide the sun onto the shaft with its flat against the shaft flat and the hub resting on the pilot. Tighten the M3 set screw through the hub hole onto the flat.
3. Press an M3 x 18 screw from below into each pin hole of the rear plate, so the heads sit in the counterbores. Hold the plate with the heads down.
4. Slide a planet onto each pin. Herringbone teeth only mesh in the right phase. In both presets every planet sits in the same orientation relative to its arm (check against the assembly render), so turn all of them the same way: a tooth gap pointing at the sun.
5. Rotate the planets by hand until the teeth line up with the sun and the ring, then lower the whole stack so the rear plate sits in the housing and the sun hub passes through the hole in the plate. If it will not drop, turn the sun a little. Because the teeth are herringbone it should find its place without force.
6. Drop the front plate on the pins, stub up. Press an M3 nut into each hex trap and thread it onto the pin. Turn the output stub by hand; the sun should follow at four turns for one turn of the output.
7. Push the bearing into the cover pocket from the top. Put the cover on the housing and secure it with four M3 x 16 screws.
8. Slide the flange over the stub, collar down, against the bearing inner race. Tighten its set screw on the stub flat.

Check: ten turns of the input by hand should move the output 2.5 turns. If something rubs once per turn, a plate is touching the housing wall; file the plate rim.
